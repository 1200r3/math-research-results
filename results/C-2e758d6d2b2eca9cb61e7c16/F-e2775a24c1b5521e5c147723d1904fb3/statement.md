# Exact statement

For each integer n >= 1, let T_n be the set of pairs (i,j) of nonnegative integers with i+j <= n-1, embedded using unit basis vectors at angle 60 degrees. A coloring with at most k colors is a map to {0,...,k-1} for which no three distinct points at equal pairwise distance all receive the same color. This excludes monochromatic equilateral triangles of every orientation and side length.

Let f(n) denote the least k admitting such a coloring. Then:

1. No proper coloring of T_9 with at most three colors exists.
2. T_12 has a proper coloring with at most four colors.
3. Consequently f(9)=f(10)=f(11)=f(12)=4, and f(n)>=4 for all n>=9.

Part 1 answers Question 2 of Brouwer, Joe, Noble, and Noble (arXiv:2405.12321v1, Section 2). Part 2 lowers the original upper bound f(12)<=5. The finite statements are verified by exhaustive computation; no assertion about four-colorability of T_13 is made.
