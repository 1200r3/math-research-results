**B-TATAMI-01 — Definitions, parity, and literature input** (`one-monomino-stabilization.md`, Literature input; TeX/PDF §§1–2). One monomino and dominoes imply odd area. If `d` is odd, exactly one of `r,r+d` is even. If `d` is even, odd area requires odd `r`; this covers all parity exclusions, even for `r<5`. The 2-by-2 cell condition in [Alhazov, Morita and Iwamoto](https://www.kurims.kyoto-u.ac.jp/~kyodo/kokyuroku/contents/pdf/1691-01.pdf) §2 is the same grid condition: a vertex between four cells is legal precisely when at least one incident cell-pair edge is crossed by a domino. §6 explicitly counts reflected or rotated tilings separately when distinct. There `t_1` counts *at most* one monomino, but on odd area it is exactly one, and §7 takes `t'=t_1−t` for exactly one. §§3.2, 5–6 put the fixed smaller side first. All applications in the claim with odd area have `w≥r≥5`, so the exceptional height-3 block degeneration does not enter.

The formulas in §7 on p. 7 give `A=(1−P)^{-1}`, `B=4(z^(r−2)+z^r)A` and `C=2(z+z^r)A²`, with `P=z^(r−1)+z^(r+1)`. They agree with the listed block widths and the displayed combined numerator in §7. In §6, the printed `w−r−2` offset would give a width `r+2` corner block; `w−r+2` is the one for the width `r−2` block used by §7. The `r≥w` in §7's introduction conflicts with its displayed sequence starting at `w=r` and increasing `w`, as well as the orientation in §§3.2 and 5–6. The manuscript uses the supported widths and range. The exact deductions also use only near-square widths `r<w≤2r−3`: in this range `M_r` plus a pure block and `L_r` plus two pure blocks are already too wide. The corner conclusion at gap 2 needs only the `L_r` arrangement with one pure block, so the single column is at one side and its monomino at one end is an outer corner.

**B-TATAMI-02 — Coefficients and multiplicities** (TeX/PDF §3). `P` has positive exponents `r−1,r+1`, while any product of two or more factors of `P` has exponent at least `2r−2`. The four offsets at width `r+d` are `d+2,d` for `B`, and `r+d−1,d` for `C`. For `d=2`, the first `C` offset is `r+1<2r−2` for every `r≥5`. It is obtained by one long block; the two positions of the single `L_r` relative to that block and the factor 2 give four. For odd `r≥7`, the other offsets `4,2,2` are too small. The two axis reflections preserve the labelled-grid conditions and act transitively on its four corners. Since all four tilings put the monomino at a corner, each corner has exactly one. At `r=5` the offset 4 in `B` is the short pure block length and contributes a second four; the other offsets remain impossible.

For even `d≥4` and odd `r≥d+5`, `0<d≤d+2<r−1`, while `r+1<r+d−1≤2r−6<2r−2`. Thus all coefficients vanish. At `r=d+3≥7` the first `B` offset alone equals `r−1`, giving four. The other short offsets are `d=r−3`, and the first `C` offset is `2r−4`, strictly between `r+1` and `2r−2`. Here `w=2d+3`, as claimed.

**B-TATAMI-03 — Minimal thresholds and conjecture scope** (statement, TeX/PDF §§1, 4). In both even-gap cases `r=d+3` is admissible and differs from the eventual count, and every larger admissible size has the eventual count. This proves the least admissible anchor is `d+5` and, with the inclusive condition `r≥cutoff` on admissible terms, the least unrestricted cutoff is `d+4`. It gives 7 and 6 for `d=2`. For clarity in future editing, the manuscript could use the explicit inequality `r≥cutoff` in place of “beyond which”; the statement's lower-bound definition and the proof fix the meaning. [Erickson et al., published article](https://www.combinatorics.org/ojs/index.php/eljc/article/download/v18i1p109/pdf/), §4.1, Conjecture 3, tests parity on `r` and does not explicitly require parity of `r_0`. With the actual right-hand count at `r_0`, the literal minimum is still 8 when `d=4,m=1`: its area is even and its count is 0, while `r=7` has count 4 and all later counts are 0. With an admissible anchor the minimum becomes 9, the experimental expression in the source. [Erickson and Schurch](https://arxiv.org/abs/1110.5103) supplies the separately cited square case. None of these deductions claims general fixed-m stabilization. The proof Markdown's comparison with an earlier submitted rule is outside the exact claim and is not an input here.

**B-TATAMI-04 — Independent finite checks.** [independent_check.py](independent_check.py) and [independent_check.log](independent_check.log) reproduce the coefficient arithmetic via binomial counts of short/long block sequences and independently count tilings by filling rows of a labelled grid. Each grid filling is counted once by processing the first uncovered cell. At each new row, a vertex with no covering domino from the upper row or a vertical incoming domino forces a horizontal domino in the new row. This enforces every internal vertex, and the state tracks exactly one monomino, distinguishing each corner and noncorner positions. The two counts agree on all 60 rectangles with odd `r=5..13` and `d=1..12`; for example `(5,7)` gives two per corner, `(7,9)` gives one per corner, `(7,11)` gives four and `(9,13)` gives zero. The independent coefficient check also covers each even `d=2..100`, its last exception and the first eleven admissible tail sizes. These bounded checks supplement the proof using the cited classification; they are not used to extrapolate the tail. I found no open mathematical issue.
