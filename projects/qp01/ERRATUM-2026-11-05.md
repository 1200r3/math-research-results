# QP01: numerical correction to the comparison with Example 4.6

The positivity conclusion in [Gajdzica, Example 4.6](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40993-024-00532-7) holds. For the expression printed there, the exceptional class and the leading constant require correction.

Let \(p_A\) have generating function \((1-z)^{-4}(1-z^{300})^{-1}\), as in the source. Its displayed
\(h_A(n)=3p_A(n+2)^2-4p_A(n+1)p_A(n+3)+p_A(n)p_A(n+4)\)
is half the full transform \(T_2(p_A)(n)\). The QP01 boundary formula, with \(d=2\), \(m=300\), and \(\binom42=6\), gives

\[
h_A(n)=\frac{1+150\,\mathbf{1}_{300\mid n+4}}{1080000}\,n^4+O(n^3).
\]

| Residue of \(n\) mod 300 | Actual leading coefficient of \(h_A\) |
|---|---:|
| 296 | \(151/1080000\) |
| All other residues, including 297 | \(1/1080000\) |

The offset \(n+4\) also follows from the fourth forward difference of \(p_A\), which is \(\mathbf{1}_{300\mid n+4}\). The printed lower-bound coefficient \(1/(2^3\cdot3^3\cdot5^4)=1/135000\) is eight times the ordinary coefficient; it cannot hold asymptotically on the ordinary classes for the displayed \(h_A\). In particular, changing only 297 to 296 would leave a second numerical error.

The [Python check](code/check_example46.py) evaluates nine exact points per residue. Each shifted partition count is a polynomial of degree at most four on a fixed residue by summation of a cubic, so the transform has degree at most eight there. The [output](results/example46-coefficients.json) shows degree four and a positive leading coefficient on all 300 residues. The [Lean regression](lean/Example46Regression.lean) specializes the previously accepted boundary coefficient and explicitly checks 296 and 297. Neither the eventual strict positivity classification nor the above-threshold failure theorem changes; the original fixed record remains in effect.
